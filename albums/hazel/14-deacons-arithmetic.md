# 14. Deacon's Arithmetic
**Artist:** Hazel · **Featuring:** Rook · **Narrator:** Rook · **Lane:** Knock · 144 BPM · Ebm

## Story Concept
Monday mornings at 8, a folding table in the fellowship hall at Diagonal Ave Baptist. Mother Ruth has counted the Sunday plate for thirty-one years, but her knuckles are too swollen now, so I run the adding machine. Envelope #1 is Deacon Bishop's own tithe: two years of the same soft, folded $40. Then comes the first Sunday after the Rooks raid, and #1 holds $400 in brand-new twenties with sequential serial numbers and a bank-band crease. Street money is soft, and somebody printed his. I take out the ledger and put my "chaplain" column (his ten percent, $11,240 over two years) beside Grandmama's saved church bulletins, "Saturday Prayer & Meditation, Private Counsel." My "code" column shows nine raids in two years, and every one landed within seventy-two hours of a Saturday King Reese knelt at that rail. Then there's a gray sedan with a thumb-shaped dent idling by the church dumpster, its plate hidden by the lid, and the same dent in April in the courthouse row marked FEDERAL VEHICLES ONLY on the day of Tre's sentencing. I copy that plate. I can't mail any of it to county, so the night Knight comes home I lay out one page in green ink, four lines long. He says "Not the Deacon, he baptized Tre," and folds it into his wallet. The turn is Rook's: the other half of the proof was penciled on his ninth-floor wall the whole time, the plate from the church dumpster. It matches mine. It takes a bullet, nine days in the ICU and Agent Cole's transcript before Knight lays page one in the collection plate (STALEMATE's "Discovered Attack"), with my green page folded behind it where only the Deacon will see.
