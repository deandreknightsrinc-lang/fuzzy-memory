# 06. Diagonal Ave
**Artist:** Hazel · **Featuring:** none · **Narrator:** Rook · **Lane:** Bounce · 98 BPM · F# minor

## Story Concept
A Saturday in late August at Pearl's Press & Curl on the west side: hot combs hissing on the stove, pressing grease and gossip in the air, the whole shop leaning into Hazel's business. Her girls warn her: Tonya, from her nursing cohort, asks if she knows what a gunshot wound looks like from both ends of a boy's life, and Nita, the choir soprano, says "he east side" like it's a diagnosis. Hazel answers the room: the pipe that took her mother was sold on Diagonal Ave, both sides of it, and a man who can wait eight hours at a payphone for a "no" is a man who can learn. At seven o'clock she walks past Deacon Bishop on the church steps (his smile holds one beat too long, and she notices) and crosses the avenue that cuts the 64 in two, straight across to Knight on the yellow line at the bus stop, and for the first time in her life she doesn't count the cars. Rook takes the bridge from the ninth floor: the Bishop moves on a slant, the Knight comes sideways, the Rook only goes straight, but "Queens can move any direction. That's what scares the other pieces." Hazel ends by claiming the choice as hers alone.
