# 07. Big Otis
**Artist:** Hazel · **Featuring:** Big Otis · **Narrator:** Rook · **Lane:** Bounce · 100 BPM · E minor

## Story Concept
A Saturday in September at Monroe Tire & Brake, back of Seventh by the tracks, where the freight trains rattle the glass. Big Otis has been home since April after twelve years upstate, and Mother Ruth sends Hazel over with a plate of smothered chicken under foil. She notices Deacon Bishop's church van on the lift with the back seats pulled out, riding low. Then she notices Knight's Cutlass parked crooked by the fence, and through the cracked office door she hears her father set a .38 on a steel desk beside a photo of her at nine in Easter lace, and give Knight three bullets: one for the man coming for him, one for him, and one for whoever is standing next to him. She walks in with the plate and the rage: *you don't get to protect me now.* Otis answers in the only language he has, a confession. He sold what took Lorraine, the funeral letter reached him nine days late in the hole, and he answered one letter in four because a stamp cost two days' pay at eleven cents an hour. He kept all hundred and four green letters in a rubber band. She lets him hold the door but not her, and there is no hug. On the ride home she opens the Cutlass ashtray for a mint, finds the three bullets, and Knight's silence becomes the first lie he tells her.
