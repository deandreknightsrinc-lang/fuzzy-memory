Desk ↔ road case

Two ways to live: set up as an L-shaped studio desk, or closed up as two rolling road cases and a top pack. Nothing is left over in either mode: every part has a job in both.

Build it

The order matters: laminate and flatten every panel first, build the two cases, then the tops, then finish. Strength comes from three things: layers glued with their corrugation crossing, ribs inside the tops, and glued corner blocks.

    Before you power it on

    Heat and fire

    • A computer inside a box needs a path for air: intake in the door, exhaust out the back. Keep the back of the Tower Case at least 3 in off the wall.
    • Cardboard burns. Keep the power strip on the open shelf, not shut in a cubby, and never put a space heater or lamp against the cases.
    • A fire-retardant spray for paper and fabric adds a margin of safety; it does not make cardboard fireproof.
    • Watch the computer's temperatures for the first week (CPU and GPU should stay where they were outside the case). Add a 120 mm fan to the door if they climb.

    Weight and water

    • Load-test each top with books before trusting it with gear, adding weight a little at a time and watching for sag.
    • Put heavy things (speakers, the computer monitor arm) over the cases, not in the middle of a span.
    • Seal every surface. Cardboard that gets wet loses most of its strength; no drinks on the desk until it is sealed.
    • Don't stand or sit on the desk.
