Magic Words: Can What You Say Help You Get Your Way?

By Apostle Sir DeAndre Knight

The Magic Spell Hypothesis

Researchers tested their hypothesis by asking 200 non-lawyers to write laws and stories about crimes. The participants naturally used convoluted structures when writing laws prohibiting crimes such as drunk driving, burglary, arson, and drug trafficking, but opted for plain English in their fiction stories. The pattern held true even when participants were asked to revise their drafts.

Instead, the study supports what the researchers call the "magic spell hypothesis." Just as spells are distinguished by a unique style that sets them apart from everyday language, legalese seems to convey a similar sense of formality and power. "People seem to understand that there's an implicit rule that this is how laws should sound, and they write them that way," says Edward Gibson, an MIT professor of brain and cognitive sciences and the senior author of the study.

In a 2022 study, the same team examined 3.5 million words from legal contracts, comparing them with movie scripts, newspaper articles, and academic papers. Their analysis uncovered a common pattern in legal documents: lengthy definitions often interrupt sentences, a structure known as "center-embedding."

In another series of experiments, around 80 participants were asked to draft laws and then write descriptions explaining those laws to visitors from another country. When crafting the laws they consistently used center-embedded structures, but avoided that phrasing in the explanations. This switching points to the idea that legalese is more about signaling authority than giving clarity.

The Word: "Let There Be Light"

Genesis 1:3. And God said, "Let there be light." The Power of Words: how language shapes reality.

By Apostle Sir DeAndre Knight

In the digital age, where communication happens in an instant and words can travel across the globe in seconds, the importance of language has never been more pronounced. Words, often seen as mere vessels for communication, carry immense power. They can shape perceptions, influence emotions, and even alter reality. This has led some to liken words to spells, highlighting their ability to manifest thoughts, desires, and intentions into tangible outcomes.

The Impact of Words on Thought and Behavior

Words are not just passive tools; they actively shape the way we think and behave. This concept, known as linguistic relativity or the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis, suggests that the language we use influences our perception of the world. Similarly, positive affirmations, simple repeated phrases, can lead to a more optimistic mindset, while negative language can foster self-doubt and anxiety.

The words we choose in our daily interactions can either build bridges or burn them. Compliments and encouragement can uplift someone, reinforcing their confidence and well-being. Conversely, harsh criticism or derogatory language can cause deep emotional wounds, sometimes lasting a lifetime. This is why words are often described as having the power to heal or harm.

Words as Spells: The Art of Manifestation

The idea that words are like spells is rooted in the belief that language can bring about real-world changes. This concept is not new; it has been a cornerstone of spiritual and philosophical traditions for centuries. In many cultures words are seen as sacred, with prayers and declarations believed to invoke divine forces.

The logic is simple: by repeatedly verbalizing a goal or outcome, one aligns their thoughts and actions with that desired reality. Phrases like "speak it into existence" or "what you say is what you get" are common. The words we use reflect our inner beliefs, and by consciously choosing our language we can steer our lives in the direction we want.
